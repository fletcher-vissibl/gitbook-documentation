# Integrated Management System (IMS)

### What is an IMS?

An Integrated Management System combines two or more management system standards into a single, unified system. Instead of running ISO 9001, ISO 14001, and ISO 45001 as separate programs with duplicated processes, you manage everything together.

Most Vissibl customers running multiple frameworks operate an IMS.

### Why integrate?

* **Less duplication.** Shared processes like document control, internal audits, and management reviews are managed once, not three times.
* **Simpler audits.** A combined audit covers multiple standards in a single visit instead of three separate audits.
* **Lower costs.** Less paperwork, fewer meetings, less consultant time.
* **Better risk management.** Quality, environmental, and safety risks are considered together rather than in silos.
* **One way of working.** Your team follows one system, not three.

### How Vissibl handles an IMS

All three standards (9001, 14001, 45001) use the same high-level structure called Annex SL. This means many clauses are identical or very similar across standards.

In Vissibl:

* Your **Frameworks** section shows each standard separately with its own readiness score, but shared clauses are recognised across frameworks.
* **Documentation** is organised by framework, but a single document (like a management review procedure) can be linked to multiple framework requirements.
* **Risk Assessment**, **Non Conformities**, **Management Review**, and other sections all work across your full IMS rather than being siloed by standard.
* The **AI Company Context** questionnaire you complete in Settings feeds context into all your frameworks at once.

### Tips

* If you're new to IMS, start by getting one framework solid (usually ISO 9001) before adding others. The shared structure means the second and third frameworks are significantly less work.
* When documenting processes, think about where quality, environment, and safety overlap. A single procedure for document control, for example, can serve all three standards.
